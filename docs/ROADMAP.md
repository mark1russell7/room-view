# Roadmap

This file is the plan of room-view. The Status page of the site reads it, and the tracker of the project mirrors it.

| ID | Item | Status | Summary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | The room format and the scene format | done | The records `room/1` and `scene/1`: walls, openings, floors, outlets, circuits, placed objects and loads. The schemas examine the form, and the checks examine the meaning. `@mark1russell7/layout-contract` 0.10.2 has the 3D points, boxes and transforms. |
| R2 | The first package: the model and the viewer | done | `@mark1russell7/room-view` makes the 3D model of a room from its measurements: walls with corners, openings, floors, outlets and placed objects. The entry `/three` shows the model with three.js: a person turns the room, the walls in front become clear, and a click selects a record. The site shows a sample room, and a browser with no WebGL 2 gets its plan. Version 0.1.0 is on npm since 2026-11-16. |
| R3 | Outlets and circuits | done | The viewer shows each outlet with a marker. The color and the shape of the marker tell the load of its circuit against its limit. A marked outlet shows a line to each machine on it. The site shows the circuits as meters, with a legend of the signs. |
| R4 | Compare scenes | open | Sliders and charts change a scene, and the viewer compares two scenes. |
| R5 | The glTF export | open | A function writes the model of a room as a glTF file, with the correct units and names, for other tools. The model comes from measurements, so a Blender path is not necessary. |
| R6 | A survey form | in progress | A person with a tape measure knows lengths, not coordinates. The record `survey/1` has the length of each wall in the sequence of a walk, and the turn at each corner. `expandSurvey` makes the `room/1` record. It shares a small gap of the walk between the sides, and it tells a mistake. The page "Measure a room" of the site is the form: a person types the numbers, and the page draws the room and shows it in 3D. Each wall takes its doors, its windows, its openings and its outlets, and the page tells a feature that does not fit. The page keeps a list of rooms, and it shows the state of each room. The numbers stay in the browser. The record `level/1` has the rooms of an apartment and the doors that join them. `expandLevel` gives each room its place from a door, and it makes one room record of the apartment. The page joins rooms: at a door, a person selects the door of the other room that is the same door. The view All rooms draws the rooms together, and it tells if the rooms fit at each door. With All rooms selected, a file has each room and the doors between them. A survey also has objects: a person measures a desk or a rack from a wall, and a display from the edge of its desk. `expandSurvey` and `expandLevel` give the placed objects of a scene. The form takes the furniture and the equipment of each wall, and the things on them or in them. The page draws them from above and in 3D, and it tells an object that is not in the room, in a wall or in the space of a different object. The page has a list of the circuits of the electrical panel, with the breaker and the voltage of each circuit. Each outlet of a wall selects its circuit. Next: windows between rooms, and the thickness of the wall at a door. |
| R7 | A core of space and add-on layers | in progress | The core knows only space: rooms, walls, openings, floors, placed objects and anchors. Each topic is an add-on package with its records on anchors, its checks, its formulas and its marks. The add-ons do not know each other. Electrical moves out of the core into the first add-on, `@mark1russell7/electrical`, with the circuit math. The marks come from `@mark1russell7/visuals`, and procedures packages give the API as JSON. The page keeps the numbers that a person typed. The design is in [`ADD-ONS.md`](ADD-ONS.md). The core has anchors, layers and the contract of an add-on (step 2). The electrical add-on has the layer, the checks, the circuit math and `fromSceneV1` (step 3). The site reads the limits and the levels of the circuits from the add-on (the first part of step 5). The core has `layerMarks` in its entry `/marks`, and the electrical add-on gives its marks in its entry `/marks`. `cluster-design` 0.2.0 imports the circuit math from the add-on (step 4). The core and the add-on are 0.2.0 on `@mark1russell7/visuals` 0.3. A circuit with no limit gives a meter with no limit (`@mark1russell7/electrical` 0.3.0). The viewer is on the stage and the markers of `@mark1russell7/visuals` (`@mark1russell7/room-view` 0.2.1): its option `marks` draws the marks of the layers, and the signs of the outlets of a `scene/1` are marks too. The core has the form of a layer as data, and the electrical add-on gives the form of its layer in its entry `/form` (`@mark1russell7/room-view` 0.2.2, `@mark1russell7/electrical` 0.3.2). The store of the measure page keeps the records of each add-on, and the page makes its inputs from the forms of the add-ons (step 5). A survey and a level have the field `layers`, and `expandSurvey` and `expandLevel` carry the layers to the room record (`@mark1russell7/room-view` 0.2.3, `@mark1russell7/electrical` 0.3.3, the first part of step 6). The form keeps a field that names a record outside its layer (`@mark1russell7/room-view` 0.2.4). The electrical add-on moves the old records of a survey and of a level to layers (`@mark1russell7/electrical` 0.3.4). Next: the page writes the records of each add-on as layers, and the site gives the marks of the layers to the viewer. Then the core removes its electrical fields (the last part of step 6). |
